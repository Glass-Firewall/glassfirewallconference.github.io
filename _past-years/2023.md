## The first Glass Firewall conference

The inaugural Glass Firewall Conference brought women together at GitHub headquarters in San Francisco, with a virtual option for attendees joining from elsewhere. About 60 people came to the free event created by Capital One, GitHub, and HackerOne, and for many of them it was the first time they had been in a room full of women learning security together.

The program was built for beginners and early-career professionals, with sessions on HackerOne, web vulnerabilities, reconnaissance, Burp Suite, and a web application capture-the-flag challenge. It created a space where people could ask questions without feeling like they had to already know the answers.

## Why the event is needed

Glass Firewall began because women are consistently underrepresented in bug bounty programs and security research, and the path into ethical hacking often feels difficult to enter without years of prior experience. The first conference made that path feel more open: attendees could learn the basics, meet women already working in the field, and start building a network of people who understood what it was like to begin from scratch.

It also set the tone for the events that followed: teach practical skills, share honest career stories, and create a place where people can practice, connect, and belong.

## Community support

Community partners included the Women’s Society of Cyberjutsu, Black Girls Hack, Women in CyberSecurity, and Women in Security and Privacy. They helped spread the word and connected attendees with other groups supporting women in security.
